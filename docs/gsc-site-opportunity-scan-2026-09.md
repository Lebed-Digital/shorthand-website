**Companion docs — read these too, don't treat this scan as standalone:**

- `docs/seo-90-day-plan.md` — the active execution plan (Jul 18–Oct 16, 2026) with dated project status.

**Critical caveat inherited from `seo-query-ownership.md` (2026-07-30 finding, still true):** all figures in this document are **blended worldwide** unless stated otherwise. This site's US conversion rate is roughly 13x the rest-of-world rate (GA4, 90d to 2026-07-30: 30 US signups vs. 2 non-US on comparable organic volume), and blended position/CTR numbers can both overstate and understate a page's real US commercial value in either direction — e.g. `report-card-comments-for-behavior` read position 8.8 blended but 40.0 US-only as of that date; `best-parent-communication-apps-for-documentation-2026` looked dead at US-only position 41.6 but was actually at position 13.2 unfiltered, because GSC's country filter has a known undercount/scramble artifact on this domain (see `reference_gsc-country-filter-artifact` in Brain memory). **Use this scan for reach and content-strategy questions. Do not use it alone to size US commercial value — re-segment carefully, with the artifact in mind, before making a funnel decision.**

## 10. Unexpected winners that do not fit current ShortHand positioning

**`/blog/welcome-letter-to-parents-from-teacher`, `/blog/short-welcome-message-to-parents-from-teacher`, `/blog/teacher-introduction-letter-to-parents`** — combined ~1,336 clicks, the largest traffic source on the entire site, and none of it is behavior-documentation or parent-communication-log content, ShortHand's actual product positioning. **`/blog/best-classdojo-alternatives-2026` and its comparison siblings** — strong traffic, and closer to product fit (these are teachers actively comparing classroom apps), but the searcher's stated need (points/rewards systems, parent messaging broadcast) is not exactly what ShortHand does (individual behavior/documentation tracking). ## F. SaaS marketing site vs. teacher content site vs. hybrid

**Recommendation: hybrid, but a hybrid with an explicit, currently-implicit split between "top of funnel" and "product-fit" content, and the site's own traffic data is already behaving like one without anyone having decided it on purpose.**

The evidence:

- The site's two largest traffic clusters by a wide margin (welcome/introduction letters at ~1,336 clicks, ClassDojo comparisons at ~321 clicks) are **not primarily behavior-documentation or parent-communication-log content** — they're generic teacher back-to-school needs and general classroom-app comparison shopping. This is classic top-of-funnel content-site behavior: broad teacher pain points, high volume, weaker immediate product fit.
- The site's product-adjacent content (report-card comments, parent behavior emails, IEP/MTSS documentation) is smaller in raw clicks but sits much closer to what ShortHand actually does, and the IEP/MTSS pages show the best CTR on the site, suggestive (not proven, small sample) of a more qualified, higher-intent audience.
- The pages built to be pure SaaS landing pages (parent-communication-log tool/feature pages, report-card-comment-generator) are the biggest underperformers on the entire site relative to their purpose. If getshorthandapp.com were meant to function primarily as a SaaS marketing site, these pages succeeding would be the clearest signal of it working — instead they're nearly invisible.
- `docs/seo-query-ownership.md` already documents a deliberate hybrid choice made in practice: the ClassDojo roundup routes grading/lesson-planning searchers to other tools rather than force-fitting them to ShortHand, explicitly trading a possible AI Overview mention for editorial honesty. That is a content-site instinct (serve the searcher's real need) operating inside a SaaS company's site.

**What this means practically:** the site is already a hybrid in outcome, just not in stated strategy. The recommendation is to make that explicit rather than to change direction:
- Keep investing in the broad teacher-content clusters (welcome letters, ClassDojo comparisons, report-card comments) as top-of-funnel reach and domain-authority builders — they are genuinely working and growing fast.
- Treat the product-specific SaaS landing pages (parent-communication-log, report-card-comment-generator) as a **separate workstream with a different success metric.** Their failure mode isn't a content problem this scan can fix; it's most likely indexing, internal linking, or on-page structure, and deserves a dedicated technical SEO audit rather than more blog content pointed at the same unindexed pages.
- Resist the temptation to retrofit the high-traffic content clusters into harder product pitches. The evidence in the ownership doc (AI Overviews citing competitors, not ShortHand, on the app-comparison queries; the deliberate decision not to "rewrite the roundups to promote ShortHand harder") suggests that the neutrality of the top-of-funnel content is plausibly what makes it rank and get cited at all. Protect that; build product-conversion muscle in the SaaS-page layer and in on-page CTAs within the content, not by degrading the content's objectivity.
- The open, unresolved question this scan cannot answer is **whether the top-of-funnel traffic is actually converting to signups/trials at an acceptable rate.** That is a GA4/funnel question, not a GSC question, and per `seo-query-ownership.md`'s own standing caution about blended-vs-US data, needs careful segmentation before anyone concludes the content strategy is "working" in a business sense rather than just a traffic sense.
